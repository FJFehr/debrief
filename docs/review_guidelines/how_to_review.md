**Reason to accept -** EVEN IF YOU HATE IT. State the best parts too. Contribution. an engineering solution, a literature review, a useful artifact (a model, software, a resource), reproduction of prior results, analysis (of models or data), theory, argumentation/position. Performance or math is neither necessary nor sufficient. What did we learn that we didnt before? What did we do that we couldnt before? Are the research **claims clearly articulated** and sufficiently **backed up** by the appropriate evidence. If a claim is performance then are baselines sufficient?

**Reason to reject -**  low-quality papers are identified quite reliably. Claims not supported? presented as conclusions rather than as hypotheses/discussion. methodological flaws - only best run? errors in implementation, proofs or analysis. Insufficient information ? lack of clarity about what the research question is? what was done, why, and what was the conclusion?

What should each section have?

The introduction - clearly set out the research argument and the literature gaps. Convince me the problem exists and that it hasnt been solved. Originality of our work and explains it. Convince me that its important.

Claims/aims - These should not be surprising. If they are. The introudction needs work.

Methods - Repeat enough to show that this is not due to chance? Enough detail such that it can be repeated. Enough data to make this claim?

Results + Discussion - What were the findings? What new insights were gained? What does the data show? describe the patterns then explain the significant to the wider field. Discussion is to gather all info into the whole. Mention any inconsistencies.

Conclusion - short and reflect the aims. They should not be surprising. if not evidence-based, it's appropriate to ask for them to be re-written
